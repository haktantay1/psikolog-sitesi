About

Speech brings
the subject into being.

I am a psychoanalytically oriented psychologist and researcher. At the centre of my work are the concepts of the subject, discourse and desire. I follow how these three concepts are interwoven within Jacques Lacan’s theoretical framework, both in my clinical practice and in my academic writing.

I graduated in psychology from Yalova University and completed psychoanalytically oriented clinical internships in several institutions, alongside different clinicians. I follow Lacanian practice both in Turkey and internationally.

Approach

Lacanian
Psychoanalysis

A session does not offer a solution — it opens a space. In speech, in silence and in the slippages of meaning, the subject follows the trace of their own desire. Analytic listening attends to what is said beyond what is said.

Drag

Borromean
Knot

At the foundation of Lacan’s theory lie three rings: the Real (Réel), the Symbolic (Symbolique) and the Imaginary (Imaginaire). Like a Borromean knot, they hold one another together — if one is cut, the others come undone.

Clinical practice listens for the breaks in these three dimensions. Accompanying the subject’s discourse, the analyst recognises what the symbolic order leaves out: the traces of the real.

“L’analyste ne doit pas diriger le patient, mais diriger la cure.”

01
The Real
Le Réel

What cannot be symbolised, the field before language. Trauma, recurring symptoms, overwhelming excitement — the Real seeps through the holes in the Symbolic. Clinical work explores how one can live with these leaks.

02
The Symbolic
Le Symbolique

Language, law, the big Other. The subject is born into the symbolic order and keeps sliding along the chain of signifiers. Following this slippage, analysis of discourse brings the structure of the subject’s desire to light.

03
The Imaginary
L’Imaginaire

The mirror stage, the ego, recognition and misrecognition. The analytic relation is one of the rare places where a way out of the trap of the image becomes possible — a moment in which the subject can see themselves differently.

Process

How I work

01
First session

We talk together about your expectations, your questions and your history. This session is for assessment and involves no commitment.

02
Framework

The frequency and length of sessions and the way we will work are agreed. The psychoanalytic frame creates a space in which the subject’s speech can unfold freely.

03
Analytic process

Through free association we discover the doors that open onto the unconscious. Every session is singular; what leads is not the analysand but speech itself.

04
Transformation

Psychoanalysis aims not at instant solutions but at a lasting subjective transformation. A new position is found within discourse; the process takes time, but it leaves a mark.

Lacanian notation

Mathemes

To reduce the core of his theory to formulas, Lacan used algebraic mathemes. Those below form the skeleton of the clinic and of writing. Essay on the formulas of sexuation →

$ ◇ a
Fantasy

Le fantasme — the basic pattern of the relation between the divided subject and objet a.

S1 → S2
Signifying chain

A signifier represents the subject for another signifier. Meaning slides along the chain.

S(A)
Signifier of the barred Other

The big Other is not whole; it carries a lack within its own set of signifiers.

a
Objet petit a

The object-cause of desire — a sliding void that can never be fully grasped.

$
Divided subject

The subject who has entered language, divided — living in the gap between what they say and what they mean.

Φ
Phallus

The signifier of lack; what determines desire in the symbolic order — not an organ but a function.

Lacanian theory

The Object of Desire

Objet petit a — the object-cause of desire. The void that can never be fully obtained, that slips away as soon as it is reached, around which the subject keeps turning. Lack is not the engine of desire; it is desire itself.

Concepts

Short definitions of the Lacanian and Freudian concepts used across the site. Click to open.

L’inconscientUnconscious

For Lacan, the unconscious is structured like a language. It is not simply a storehouse of the repressed; it works in the cut between what is said and what cannot be said. Read the essay →

$Divided subject

The person who has entered language, divided. They live in the gap between what they say and what they mean; this is exactly where the unconscious speaks. Read the essay →

Le RéelThe Real

What cannot be symbolised or put into words. The place where trauma, anxiety and the repeating symptom seep through. Read the essay →

Le SymboliqueThe Symbolic

Language, law, the big Other. The order into which the subject is born and in which it takes its place in a chain of sliding meanings. Read the essay →

L’ImaginaireThe Imaginary

The mirror stage, the ego, recognition and misrecognition. The imaginary order: the illusory field in which the “I” appears. Read the essay →

Objet petit aObject of desire

The object-cause that sets desire in motion and can never be fully grasped. It turns around a lack. Read the essay →

Le DésirDesire

What remains between demand and need. It does not end with a satisfaction; it always moves towards the desire of the Other. Read the essay →

Le Grand Autre (A)The big Other

The cornerstone of language and law; the symbolic order that constitutes the subject as it speaks. Not a person but a place. Read the essay →

JouissanceEnjoyment beyond pleasure

A satisfaction that goes beyond the limit of pleasure and is interwoven with pain. In Lacan, the bond between the symptom and the subject. Read the essay →

The moment when a child between six and eighteen months recognises its image in the mirror as “I”. The ego is founded from the start on a misrecognition. Read the essay →

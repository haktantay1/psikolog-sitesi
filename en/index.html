Speaking, and in which language

Psychoanalysis is a practice of speech. Someone speaks, as freely as they can, about what brings them; someone listens, not only to what is said but to the way it is said: the slip, the repetition, the word that comes back from another time. The aim is not to hand out advice or to correct thoughts, but to open a space in which a person can hear something of their own desire that they had not been able to hear before.

For people who live between languages, the question of which language to speak in is not a detail. Some find that certain things can only be said in the language of childhood; others find that a second language gives them the distance they need to say anything at all. In sessions you can choose English, French or Turkish, and you can move between them. The movement itself is often part of the work.

I work with people for whom this matters in different ways: English and French speakers living in Turkey; people living abroad who would rather not do this work in the local language; Turkish speakers in Europe and beyond (there is a separate page in Turkish on this); and readers of Freud and Lacan who want to know what analysis is from the inside.

How it works

The first session is free and carries no commitment. We talk about what brings you, what you expect and what questions you have. It is also the moment to settle the practical frame together: how often we meet, the length of sessions, fees, and the platform we will use.

Times are arranged across time zones. Turkey keeps UTC+3 all year, so Istanbul is two hours ahead of Paris or Berlin in winter and one hour in summer, and three hours ahead of London in winter and two in summer.

Insurance. Health insurers outside Turkey generally do not reimburse sessions with a practitioner based abroad. If this matters to you, please check with your insurer before we begin.

Confidentiality is the condition of the work. Please do not send detailed personal or clinical information by email; we can talk about it in the first session.

These sessions are not an emergency service. If you are in danger or thinking of ending your life, please call your local emergency number (112 in the European Union and Turkey, 999 in the United Kingdom, 911 in North America) or a crisis line where you live: Samaritans on 116 123 in the UK and Ireland, 988 in the United States and Canada.

About

Alongside clinical work I write and speak on psychoanalysis. Questions

Do I need to speak Turkish?

No. Sessions can take place entirely in English or in French. If you speak more than one of these languages, you are free to move between them.

I live outside Turkey. Can I still start?

Yes. Sessions take place online, and times are arranged to suit your time zone. Turkey keeps UTC+3 all year round.

Is online psychoanalysis really psychoanalysis?

The frame changes; the work of speech does not. Psychoanalysis has always had to adapt its setting, and what matters is that the frame is agreed, kept and respected by both sides. We discuss it in the first session.

How long does it last, and what does it cost?

There is no fixed length; psychoanalytic work takes the time it takes, and you can stop when you choose. Fees and frequency are discussed in the first session, which is free.

Will my health insurance reimburse the sessions?

In most countries, insurers do not reimburse sessions with a practitioner based abroad. Please check with your insurer if this matters to you.

Do you offer supervision or academic help?

Yes. I offer clinical supervision and academic consultation for students and researchers working on Freud, Lacan and psychoanalytic theory, in English, French or Turkish.

To arrange a first session, or to ask about supervision or academic consultation, write to me in English, French or Turkish. It helps if you mention your time zone.
